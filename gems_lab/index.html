GEMS Lab

Experimental science is not rich science.

01

The series

Most science videos show someone else doing the experiment. GEMS Lab doesn't. We stopped filming ourselves because watching a demonstration quietly tells students the experiment is something other people do. Instead, every GEMS Lab video hands the experiment to you — the materials, the question, the method — and then explains the physics once you've seen it with your own hands.

Every one of the 40 experiments uses household or very low-cost materials, because the point of the series is in its name: all of us can be researchers, right here and right now.

02

How the three phases work

1

Experience the science

The video walks you through the procedure and materials. You follow along, run the experiment, and meet the concept for the first time.

2

Scaffold the science

A short concept explanation comes first. Then you're guided to design your own procedure, choose your own variables, and predict the result before testing it.

3

Build the science

You print the lab report and run the investigation on your own. The video becomes your answer key, with hints along the way — not a script.

Growth, not repetition. Each phase removes a little more of the scaffolding until the student is the scientist.
